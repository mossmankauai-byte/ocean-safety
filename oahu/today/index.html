North Shore

Waimea Bay ⚠
Summer it can be calm and swimmable, but from roughly Oct–Apr Waimea sees giant NW swell, a violent sand-bottom shorebreak that breaks necks, and strong rip currents.
ʻEhukai Beach (Banzai Pipeline) ⚠
Pipeline/Backdoor breaks over a shallow, jagged reef just feet from shore.
Sunset Beach ⚠
Notorious for powerful, fast-moving rip currents (the ‘Sunset rip’) and large winter NW surf with hard shorebreak.
Haleʻiwa Aliʻi Beach Park
The most sheltered, family-oriented North Shore beach park and the safest local swim option, especially in summer — but it is still North Shore: respect the harbor channel current and never swim when winter surf is up.
Sharks Cove (Pūpūkea) ⚠
Part of the Pūpūkea Marine Life Conservation District — superb summer snorkeling, but the sharp lava-rock entry and exposure mean even moderate swell turns it deadly.
Laniākea Beach ⚠
Treat as no lifeguard.
Three Tables ⚠
Part of the Pūpūkea MLCD with no dedicated lifeguard at this spot.
Chun's Reef ⚠
Treat as no lifeguard and a shallow reef bottom.

Windward (East)

Lanikai Beach ⚠
The shoreline itself is calm and reef-shielded, BUT there is no lifeguard and the water off Lanikai toward the Mokulua islets is deadly.
Kailua Beach Park
Guarded, gently shelving sand and usually mild — Hawaii's classic family bay.
Kalama Beach
The calmer, less-crowded north end of Kailua Bay with its own guard tower (opened 2024).
Waimānalo Bay Beach Park
Longest white-sand beach on Oʻahu and guarded — lifeguards often steer visitors here as a calmer alternative to Sandy Beach.
Bellows Field Beach Park
Soft sand and a popular beginner bodyboard shorebreak, but the lifeguard is only there during daytime weekend public-access hours — there is NO guard overnight or on weekdays even though the gate may be open until midnight.
Kualoa Regional Park
Shallow, calm water inside Kāneʻohe Bay's barrier reef with Mokoliʻi (Chinaman's Hat) islet offshore — gentle for small kids at higher tide, but it goes very shallow and rocky at low tide, and the tide comes in fast if you wade out toward the islet.
Makapuʻu Beach Park ⚠
Powerful, steep dumping shorebreak with no protecting reef — one of Oʻahu's most injury-prone bodyboard beaches.

South Shore (Waikīkī)

Waikīkī Beach
Offshore reefs and breakwaters keep the swimming sections gentle most days; the famous beginner surf breaks (Canoes, Queens) sit just outside the swim zone.
Ala Moana Beach Park
A wide offshore reef and a dredged channel make this the calmest, most kid-friendly swim on the south shore; the bottom drops off fast into the old 20–30ft boat channel, so keep weak swimmers and small kids in the shallow reef flat and away from the deep channel at the ʻewa end.
Kaimana Beach (Sans Souci)
Quiet, locals-favorite end of Waikīkī below Diamond Head with calm reef-protected water; Hawaiian monk seals haul out and have pupped here, so by law give any resting seal at least 50 feet and never get between a seal and the water (a bite occurred here in 2021).
Hanauma Bay ⚠
World-class snorkeling on the protected inner reef, but powerful rip currents run through the reef channels and out the bay mouth — at least ~51 drowning deaths recorded here since 1995.
Sandy Beach ⚠
The most dangerous shorebreak on Oʻahu and reportedly the highest neck/back-injury beach in the United States ('Break Neck Beach').
China Walls (Koko Kai) ⚠
A lava cliff shelf, NOT a swimming beach, and one of the most dangerous spots on Oʻahu — rescues happen frequently, especially in the April–October south-swell season.

Leeward (West)

Ko Olina Lagoons
Four man-made, breakwater-protected lagoons — the calmest, most kid-safe water on the Leeward coast.
Pōkaʻī Bay Beach Park
A breakwater shelters the bay, making it the calmest lifeguarded swim on the Waianae coast — genuinely safe for kids most of the year.
Mākaha Beach ⚠
Powerful surf beach.
Māʻili Beach Park ⚠
Long open west-facing beach exposed to west/northwest swell.
Nānākuli Beach Park ⚠
Strong shorebreak that can dump even on summer days, and high surf with strong currents in winter.
Electric Beach (Kahe Point) ⚠
Snorkel-only site with NO safe swim entry: a rocky lava-bench shore and an unavoidable ~200-yard open-water swim out to the power-plant outflow pipes, with real currents.
Yokohama Bay (Keawaʻula) ⚠
Remote beach with a steep, dumping shorebreak, strong currents, and a rocky bottom.
