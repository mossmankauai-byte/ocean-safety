Oʻahu beaches that carry a hazard warning

  • Waimea Bay (North Shore): Summer it can be calm and swimmable, but from roughly Oct to Apr Waimea sees giant NW swell, a violent sand-bottom shorebreak that breaks necks, and strong rip currents.
  • ʻEhukai Beach (Banzai Pipeline) (North Shore): Pipeline/Backdoor breaks over a shallow, jagged reef just feet from shore. Even pro surfers die and are seriously injured here.
  • Sunset Beach (North Shore): Notorious for powerful, fast-moving rip currents (the ‘Sunset rip’) and large winter NW surf with hard shorebreak. Frequent rescues and drownings.
  • Sharks Cove (Pūpūkea) (North Shore): Part of the Pūpūkea Marine Life Conservation District, superb summer snorkeling, but the sharp lava-rock entry and exposure mean even moderate swell turns it deadly.
  • Laniākea Beach (North Shore): Treat as no lifeguard.
  • Three Tables (North Shore): Part of the Pūpūkea MLCD with no dedicated lifeguard at this spot.
  • Chun's Reef (North Shore): Treat as no lifeguard and a shallow reef bottom. Often the ‘mellow’ North Shore option, but winter swell still produces strong currents and the reef causes injuries.
  • Lanikai Beach (Windward): The shoreline itself is calm and reef-shielded, BUT there is no lifeguard and the water off Lanikai toward the Mokulua islets is deadly.
  • Makapuʻu Beach Park (Windward): Powerful, steep dumping shorebreak with no protecting reef, one of Oʻahu's most injury-prone bodyboard beaches.
  • Hanauma Bay (South Shore): World-class snorkeling on the protected inner reef, but powerful rip currents run through the reef channels and out the bay mouth.
  • Sandy Beach (South Shore): The most dangerous shorebreak on Oʻahu and reportedly the highest neck/back-injury beach in the United States ('Break Neck Beach').
  • China Walls (Koko Kai) (South Shore): A lava cliff shelf, NOT a swimming beach, and one of the most dangerous spots on Oʻahu, rescues happen frequently, especially in the April to October south-swell season.
  • Mākaha Beach (Leeward): Powerful surf beach.
  • Māʻili Beach Park (Leeward): Long open west-facing beach exposed to west/northwest swell.
  • Nānākuli Beach Park (Leeward): Strong shorebreak that can dump even on summer days, and high surf with strong currents in winter. Drownings are documented on the Nanakuli stretch of the Waianae coast.
  • Electric Beach (Kahe Point) (Leeward): Snorkel-only site with NO safe swim entry: a rocky lava-bench shore and an unavoidable ~200-yard open-water swim out to the power-plant outflow pipes, with real currents.
  • Yokohama Bay (Keawaʻula) (Leeward): Remote beach with a steep, dumping shorebreak, strong currents, and a rocky bottom.
