Oʻahu Weather Month by Month: What to Actually Expect

Oʻahu does not have four seasons, it has two: a drier summer and a wetter winter, with warm temperatures the whole way through. What changes month to month is how much rain passes over, how the ocean behaves, and which side of the island stays sunny. This guide walks through the year so you can pack and plan for what is likely, keeping in mind that the leeward south shore is always the driest.

Quick facts

  • Oʻahu has two seasons: drier summer (May to October) and wetter winter (November to April).
  • Temperatures stay roughly in the mid-70s to low 90s all year; August and September are hottest.
  • December and January are the wettest months; July is typically the driest.
  • The leeward south shore (Waikiki) is the driest and sunniest side; the windward east side gets the most rain.
  • North Shore surf is large and dangerous roughly November through March and calmest in summer.
  • Humpback whale season runs December through April, peaking January through March.

The pattern behind the numbers

Two things shape Oʻahu's weather. First, the season: summer (May to October) is drier, winter (November to April) is wetter, with December and January the rainiest. Second, geography: the windward (east) side around Kailua gets more rain and clouds, while the leeward south and west, including Waikiki, stay drier and sunnier. Temperatures barely move, generally sitting between the mid-70s and low 90s all year. So most of your planning is about rain odds and ocean conditions, not temperature.

January through March

These are among the wetter, cooler months, though "cool" here still means daytime temperatures in the low 80s. Rain comes in passing showers, heaviest on the windward and North Shore sides. This is peak humpback whale season, best January through March, and peak big-wave season on the North Shore, where surf is large and dangerous. Post-holiday January is one of the quietest, better-value stretches of the year. Pack a light rain layer and expect the south shore to stay the sunniest.

April through June

The island dries out and settles into some of its best all-around weather. April and May are shoulder months with warm days, easing crowds, and lower prices. Rain tapers off, the North Shore calms down and becomes swimmable, and south-shore surf begins its summer season. June is warm and reliably pleasant. This stretch is a strong pick if you want easy swimming and fewer crowds; check our best-time-to-visit guide for the trade-offs.

July through September

The driest, warmest part of the year, with August and September the hottest months. Days are sunny and humid, showers are brief, and most beaches around the island are at their calmest. July draws summer-holiday crowds; September eases off and is one of the better-value warm months. Bring reef-safe sunscreen and sun protection, because the UV is strong; see our reef-safe sunscreen guide. Watch the daily forecast for the small chance of a tropical system, which is most likely in this window.

October through December

October is a pleasant shoulder month that shifts toward the wet season as it ends. By November and December the rains return, the North Shore surf builds back into its dangerous winter range, and whales start arriving. December is the wettest month on average and, with the holidays, the busiest and most expensive. If you come now, favor the drier south shore for beach days and keep plans flexible around passing storms.

Which side to base yourself on

If dry, sunny beach time is your priority, base on the leeward south shore (Waikiki) or the leeward west coast, which get the least rain. The windward side is greener and more beautiful precisely because it catches more rain and cloud, so plan windward days for clearer forecasts. Whatever month you visit, conditions swing day to day and side to side, so check the live beach conditions before you head out.

Before you go

  • Warm, sunny weather does not mean calm water. From about November through March, North Shore surf is dangerous regardless of how nice the day looks.
  • Monthly averages are odds, not guarantees. Windward and North Shore rain can arrive any month, so keep plans flexible.
  • UV is strong year-round. Use reef-safe sun protection and check daily conditions before extended beach time.
