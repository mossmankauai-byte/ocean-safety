5 Days on Oʻahu: The Full-Island Itinerary

Five days lets you see every side of Oʻahu without rushing, and still leave a buffer day for weather or a beach you love enough to repeat. This plan keeps each day anchored to one region so you are not crossing the island twice a day. A rental car makes it far easier, especially for the windward and North Shore days.

Quick facts

  • Anchor each day to one region (south shore, east Honolulu, windward, North Shore, leeward) to minimize driving.
  • Diamond Head: out-of-state reservation via Go Wild up to 30 days ahead, $5 entry, $10 parking, credit card only.
  • Hanauma Bay: reservation-only, closed Monday and Tuesday, tickets release two days ahead at 7:00 a.m. HST, $25 non-resident.
  • North Shore is usually swimmable in summer and dangerous with large surf from about November through March.
  • Ko Olina lagoons and Pōkaʻī Bay on the leeward coast offer the calmest, most protected water for families.
  • Humpback whales are commonly seen December through April, with peak activity January through March.

Day 1: Ease in on the south shore

Start slow near your hotel. Waikiki is the gentlest water on the island and has lifeguards, so it is a good place to get your bearings and rent gear. Walk to the Diamond Head end for calmer, less crowded sand at Kaimana Beach. Check the live Waikiki conditions before you swim, since even here there are rip currents and boat channels. Use the evening to sort out reservations and any tour bookings for later in the week.

Day 2: Diamond Head and east Honolulu

Hike Diamond Head early. Out-of-state visitors need a reservation through the state Go Wild system, and it is $5 per person plus $10 to park. Afterward, if you booked it, Hanauma Bay is the marquee snorkel spot on this side, though it is closed Monday and Tuesday and needs a ticket reserved two days ahead. If you did not get a Hanauma slot, the beaches along the southeast coast toward Makapuʻu are a fine substitute. Check Makapuʻu and be cautious at Sandy Beach, which has a punishing shore break that injures people regularly.

Day 3: Windward coast

Cross to the windward side for Kailua and Lanikai, the postcard beaches with soft sand and, on calm days, clear water. Parking near Lanikai is residential and strictly enforced, so come early. Check Kailua Beach Park and Lanikai first. Continue up the coast past Kualoa and the fishpond views, with a stop at Waimanalo if you want a long, quiet stretch of sand. This side is windier and greener than Waikiki, and it catches more passing rain.

Day 4: North Shore

Give the North Shore a full day: Haleʻiwa town, the food trucks, turtles at Laniakea, and the big beaches at Waimea, ʻEhukai (Pipeline), and Sunset. In summer these are often calm enough to swim and snorkel. From roughly November through March they produce large, dangerous surf, and the water is for watching, not entering. Follow the lifeguards and stay back from the shore break. Read our surf season guide and check Waimea Bay before you plan to get in.

Day 5: Leeward coast or a repeat

Use the last day to fill a gap. The leeward Waiʻanae coast has calm, protected water at the Ko Olina lagoons and Pōkaʻī Bay, both good for families and easier swimming. Alternatively, go back to whichever beach you liked best, or catch anything weather cost you earlier in the week. From December through April, this coast and the leeward waters are also prime for spotting humpback whales; see our whale watching guide.

Booking and logistics

Reserve Diamond Head up to 30 days ahead and Hanauma Bay two days ahead the moment tickets release at 7:00 a.m. Hawaiʻi time. Both are credit-card only. For moving between regions, our driving times and traffic guide shows when the H-1 and Pali routes clog up. If you are not renting a car for all five days, cluster the car days around the windward and North Shore trips, which are the hardest to reach by bus.

Before you go

  • North Shore beaches have deadly winter surf and currents. From about November through March, watch from the sand and do not enter the water when surf is up.
  • Sandy Beach and other shore-break spots injure strong swimmers regularly. Respect the flags and lifeguards, and sit out heavy shore break.
  • Lanikai and Kailua parking is residential and enforced. Arrive early and read the signs to avoid a tow or ticket.
