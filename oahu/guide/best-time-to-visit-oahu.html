The Best Time to Visit Oʻahu: Season by Season

Oʻahu is a year-round destination, so the real question is what you are optimizing for: price, crowds, calm water, surf, or whales. The weather is warm every month, and the differences between seasons are more about rain, ocean conditions, and how busy the island feels. Here is how each season actually shakes out.

Quick facts

  • Weather is warm year-round, with temperatures generally between the mid-70s and low 90s.
  • Summer (May to October) is the drier season, and most beaches, including the North Shore, are calmest then.
  • Winter (November to April) is wetter, brings big dangerous surf to the north and west shores, and is whale season.
  • The best value-to-experience balance is the shoulder months: April to May and September to early October.
  • Peak crowds and prices hit around the December holidays, March spring break, and mid-summer.
  • The south shore around Waikiki is the driest, sunniest part of the island in every season.

The short answer

For the best balance of good weather, lower prices, and thinner crowds, aim for the shoulder months: mid-to-late April and May, or September and early October. These fall between the big travel peaks, so flights and hotels drop, sometimes steeply, while the weather is still warm and mostly dry. You may catch a passing shower, but you trade that for a much calmer island.

Summer (May to October): dry and calm-water season

Summer is the drier season and the time when most beaches are at their gentlest, including the North Shore, which flattens out and becomes swimmable. South-shore breaks like those off Waikiki pick up their surf in summer, so it is also the season for beginner surfing on that side. August and September are the hottest months. Crowds spike around school-holiday weeks and July 4th, then ease in September. Check our surf season guide for which shore is calm when.

Winter (November to April): rain, surf, and whales

Winter brings more rain, especially on the windward and North Shore sides, and the big-wave season on the north and west shores. From roughly November through March, North Shore beaches produce large, dangerous surf, and the water there is for watching, not swimming. On the upside, this is humpback whale season, best from December through April with peak activity January through March. The south shore around Waikiki stays the driest, sunniest part of the island year-round.

When it is busiest and most expensive

The peak periods are mid-December through early January (holidays), spring break in March, and mid-summer. Expect the highest hotel rates, fullest flights, and most competition for reservations at spots like Hanauma Bay and Diamond Head during these windows. If you must travel then, book accommodations, rental cars, and any timed-entry reservations well ahead.

When it is cheapest and quietest

The lowest prices and thinnest crowds generally land in the shoulder months (April to May and September into November) and in the quiet stretch of January after the holidays clear out. Post-holiday January is genuinely calm, though it is also cooler and wetter. If your priority is value and elbow room over guaranteed sun, these are your windows.

Matching the season to your trip

If you want easy swimming and snorkeling, favor summer, when more shores are calm. If you want to surf big waves or watch whales, come in winter and treat the North Shore as a spectator sport. If you want the best price-to-experience balance, target the shoulder months. Whatever you choose, conditions change day to day, so check the daily beach conditions and read our month-by-month weather guide before you pack.

Before you go

  • Winter surf on the north and west shores is genuinely dangerous, not just scenic. During high surf, stay out of the water and follow lifeguards and posted warnings.
  • Season tells you the odds, not the day. Always check live conditions before swimming, because calm-season beaches still have rip currents.
  • Popular timed-entry spots (Hanauma Bay, Diamond Head) sell out fastest in peak season. Reserve ahead or plan alternatives.
