How we score Maui beaches

Ocean Safe reads live ocean data and turns it into a conservative, plain-English conditions score — the same engine we use for Kauaʻi, tuned per beach by its own orientation and hazards.

Where the data comes from

Live surf (wave height, period and direction) and wind come from Open-Meteo's marine and forecast models; tides from NOAA's Kahului station. We refetch through a cached proxy so conditions never go blank under load.

How a beach is scored

Each beach carries its own compass orientation, reef shelter, shorebreak severity and lifeguard status. We compare today's swell and wind direction against the beach's exposure, then weight for the activity you're considering. A reef-sheltered, lifeguarded beach and an open, unguarded one are never judged the same way.

What we will never do

We never label a beach "safe." Conditions change in minutes, and a calm-looking beach can still have a current you can't see. Known-hazard beaches (strong shorebreak, drowning history, no safe entry) are flagged as watch-from-shore and can never score green, no matter how flat the water looks. Always check posted signs, county lifeguards, and weather.gov, and decide for yourself.
