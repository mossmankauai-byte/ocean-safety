Maui beaches that carry a hazard warning

  • Puʻu Kekaʻa (Black Rock) (West Maui): Strong current wraps around the lava point and offshore; this is one of Hawaiʻi's highest snorkel-drowning locations.
  • Honolua Bay (West Maui): Faces NNW and takes the full force of winter N/NW swell, turning into a powerful big-wave surf break (Oct to Apr) with no lifeguard, strong currents and a rocky/boulder entry.
  • Mokuleʻia Beach (Slaughterhouse) (West Maui): NNW-facing pocket beach in the Honolua to Mokuleʻia Marine Life Conservation District with no lifeguard, steep cliff stairway access and a heavy, dumping shorebreak plus rip currents on any north/NW swell (most of winter).
  • Keawakapu Beach (South Maui): No lifeguard on a long, open beach with documented snorkeling fatalities (a visitor snorkeler drowned here in Aug 2017) and rip currents that strengthen on south swells, especially toward the rocky ends and offshore.
  • Mokapu & Ulua Beach (South Maui): No lifeguard.
  • Wailea Beach (South Maui): No lifeguard on a beach with a serious recent drowning history. A snorkeler also died here in 2022, and rip-current rescues have recurred over the years.
  • Polo Beach (South Maui): No lifeguard, on the same Wailea resort coast where multiple visitor drownings have occurred at adjacent unguarded beaches (Wailea Beach, Mokapu).
  • Maluaka Beach (South Maui): No lifeguard. A 71-year-old visitor drowned while snorkeling alone off Maluaka ('Turtle Town') in Jan 2018.
  • Mākena Landing (South Maui): No lifeguard and NOT a sandy swim beach, entry is over lava rock, so this is a shore-dive and kayak launch for confident swimmers only.
  • Big Beach (Oneloa, Mākena State Park) (South Maui): Powerful dumping shorebreak that breaks directly onto a steep sand face with NO trough, it can drive swimmers headfirst into the sand.
  • Little Beach (Puʻu ʻŌlaʻi) (South Maui): No lifeguard and no vehicle access, reached only by a short, steep, slippery scramble over the lava bluff from the north end of Big Beach.
  • H.A. Baldwin Beach Park (North Shore): Lifeguarded, but the open main beach has a hard, dumping shorebreak that breaks directly on sand, a documented neck/spinal-injury hotspot ('there's a reason locals joke about a good chiropractor in Paia').
  • Sugar Cove Beach (North Shore): No lifeguard and fully open to north-shore swell.
  • Tavares Beach (North Shore): No lifeguard and a DOCUMENTED DROWNING: a 69-year-old man was found dead face-down in the Tavares Bay shorebreak (Aug 2013).
  • Kahului Harbor Beaches (Hoʻaloha / Kanahā Pond) (North Shore): This is a working commercial/cruise harbor, NOT a swimming beach, there is no safe ocean entry here.
  • Hoʻokipa Beach Park (North Shore): Expert-only water.
  • Hāmoa Beach (East/Hāna): Open-ocean pocket beach with no reef and no lifeguard.
  • Koki Beach (East/Hāna): Persistent strong rip currents and a steep, dumping shorebreak on red/dark sand, fully exposed to open-ocean swell. No lifeguard.
  • Waiʻānapanapa Black Sand Beach (Paʻiloa) (East/Hāna): No protective reef, so the water deepens abruptly at the shoreline and a powerful, violent shorebreak funnels into a small lava-rock cove with strong currents and rip.
  • Kaihalulu (Red Sand Beach) (East/Hāna): ACCESS IS THE PRIMARY KILLER: the only way in is a narrow, actively eroding cinder-cliff trail across a steep dropoff onto loose rock.
