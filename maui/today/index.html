West Maui

Kāʻanapali Beach
Long, calm leeward beach in summer; the gentlest swimming is at the south/central stretch.
Puʻu Kekaʻa (Black Rock) ⚠
Strong current wraps around the lava point and offshore; this is one of Hawaiʻi's highest snorkel-drowning locations.
Kahekili Beach Park (Airport Beach)
One of West Maui's best easy-entry snorkel reefs (part of the Kahekili Herbivore Fisheries Reserve); enter from the sandy patches and float over the reef, calmest in the morning.
Kapalua Bay
Two rocky points shelter this C-shaped crescent into one of Maui's calmest, most family-friendly swim and snorkel bays; reef and turtles are right off the sand.
Nāpili Bay
A sandy pocket bay with good morning snorkeling at the points and frequent green sea turtles; small shorebreak can form at the center when a south or west swell runs, so enter near the rocks.
D.T. Fleming Beach Park
Beautiful guarded beach (one of only two lifeguarded beaches in West Maui), but it faces more north and catches winter and NW swell that build a powerful shorebreak and rip currents — swim near the lifeguard tower and heed the flags.
Honokōwai Beach Park
A shallow, reef-sheltered wading pool that is very calm and good for small children at higher tides; it gets rocky and bottoms out at low tide, so time your visit to the tide.
Launiupoko Beach Park
A lava-rock-walled wading pool keeps the water flat and warm for toddlers, while a gentle outside break draws beginner surfers; the West Maui Mountains backdrop makes for great sunset photos.
Olowalu Beach
Shallow, calm reef known as a turtle-cleaning station, with easy entry — but Olowalu is the ONLY Maui beach with permanent shark-warning signs (a reef-shark pupping ground).
Ukumehame Beach Park
A long, shaded sand-and-pebble beach with a beginner surf break offshore (Thousand Peaks); swimming is reasonable on calm days but the bottom drops off and south swells bring shorebreak, so watch conditions.
Honolua Bay ⚠
Faces NNW and takes the full force of winter N/NW swell, turning into a powerful big-wave surf break (Oct–Apr) with no lifeguard, strong currents and a rocky/boulder entry.
Mokuleʻia Beach (Slaughterhouse) ⚠
NNW-facing pocket beach in the Honolua–Mokuleʻia Marine Life Conservation District with no lifeguard, steep cliff stairway access and a heavy, dumping shorebreak plus rip currents on any north/NW swell (most of winter).

South Maui

Kamaʻole Beach Park I
The northernmost and most protected of the three Kamaʻole parks.
Kamaʻole Beach Park II
Steeper drop-off than Kam I means slightly stronger shorebreak — fine for swimming when calm, but watch small kids at the waterline.
Kamaʻole Beach Park III
Grassy park with shade, playground and lifeguards makes this the family pick of the Kamaʻole trio.
Charley Young Beach
A quieter pocket just north of Kamaʻole I with soft golden sand and turtles on the north reef.
Keawakapu Beach ⚠
No lifeguard on a long, open beach with documented snorkeling fatalities (a visitor snorkeler drowned here in Aug 2017) and rip currents that strengthen on south swells, especially toward the rocky ends and offshore.
Mokapu & Ulua Beach ⚠
No lifeguard.
Wailea Beach ⚠
No lifeguard on a beach with a serious recent drowning history.
Polo Beach ⚠
No lifeguard, on the same Wailea resort coast where multiple visitor drownings have occurred at adjacent unguarded beaches (Wailea Beach, Mokapu).
Maluaka Beach ⚠
No lifeguard.
Mākena Landing ⚠
No lifeguard and NOT a sandy swim beach — entry is over lava rock, so this is a shore-dive and kayak launch for confident swimmers only.
Big Beach (Oneloa, Mākena State Park) ⚠
Powerful dumping shorebreak that breaks directly onto a steep sand face with NO trough — it can drive swimmers headfirst into the sand.
Little Beach (Puʻu ʻŌlaʻi) ⚠
No lifeguard and no vehicle access — reached only by a short, steep, slippery scramble over the lava bluff from the north end of Big Beach.

North Shore

Kanahā Beach Park
World-class kite/windsurf launch with a guarded swim section inside the reef.
H.A. Baldwin Beach Park ⚠
Lifeguarded, but the open main beach has a hard, dumping shorebreak that breaks directly on sand — a documented neck/spinal-injury hotspot ('there's a reason locals joke about a good chiropractor in Paia').
Baby Beach (Pāʻia)
A long offshore reef makes a shallow, flat lagoon — a popular North Shore spot for toddlers.
Spreckelsville Beach
A long, windy reef-fringed strand popular with windsurfers and kiters.
Sugar Cove Beach
A small sandy pocket at the east end of the Spreckelsville reef.
Tavares Beach ⚠
No lifeguard and a DOCUMENTED DROWNING: a 69-year-old man was found dead face-down in the Tavares Bay shorebreak (Aug 2013).
Kahului Harbor Beaches (Hoʻaloha / Kanahā Pond) ⚠
This is a working commercial/cruise harbor, NOT a swimming beach — there is no safe ocean entry here.
Hoʻokipa Beach Park ⚠
Expert-only water.

East/Hāna

Hāna Bay
The most protected, family-friendly water in the Hāna region — the breakwater and Kaʻuiki Head shelter the bay.
Hāmoa Beach ⚠
Open-ocean pocket beach with no reef and no lifeguard.
Koki Beach ⚠
Persistent strong rip currents and a steep, dumping shorebreak on red/dark sand, fully exposed to open-ocean swell.
Waiʻānapanapa Black Sand Beach (Paʻiloa) ⚠
No protective reef, so the water deepens abruptly at the shoreline and a powerful, violent shorebreak funnels into a small lava-rock cove with strong currents and rip.
Kaihalulu (Red Sand Beach) ⚠
ACCESS IS THE PRIMARY KILLER: the only way in is a narrow, actively eroding cinder-cliff trail across a steep dropoff onto loose rock.
