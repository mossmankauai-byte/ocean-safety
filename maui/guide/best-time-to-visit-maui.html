The Best Time to Visit Maui: Season by Season

Maui is a year-round destination, so the real question is what you want to trade. Winter brings whales, big north-shore surf, higher prices, and a bit more rain. Summer brings dry weather, warm water, and the biggest crowds of the year. Here is how the seasons actually shake out.

Quick facts

  • Shoulder months (April–May, late September–November) tend to offer the best mix of weather, price, and low crowds.
  • Whale season runs roughly December through April, peaking January–March.
  • December is Maui's rainiest month on average; the driest stretch is May through October.
  • Summer has the calmest, warmest water and the biggest crowds and prices, especially July.
  • Central Pacific hurricane season runs June–November, peaking August–October.

The short answer

For most first-timers, the shoulder months of April–May and September–October hit the best balance: warm dry-ish weather, thinner crowds, and lower prices than the winter and summer peaks. Come in winter if whales or big surf are the point. Come in summer if you want the calmest water and the driest coast and do not mind the crowds and rates.

Winter (December–March)

This is high season, and it earns it. Humpback whales fill the channel between Maui and Lānaʻi, peaking in January through March; see the whale-watching guide. North-shore surf is at its biggest, which is a spectacle at spots like Honolua Bay and dangerous for swimming. December is Maui's rainiest month on average, with January and February close behind. Prices and crowds spike around the December holidays. Book early.

Spring (April–May)

Spring is one of the smartest windows. The last whales trail off in April, rain eases as the island moves toward its dry season, and crowds thin out once spring break ends. Water and air are warm, and rates sit below the winter and summer peaks. If you want good weather without the highest prices, this is the stretch to target.

Summer (June–October)

Summer is the driest, most reliably sunny time on the coast, and the ocean is often at its calmest and warmest. That makes it the easiest season for snorkeling and beach days with kids. It is also the busiest, especially July, with the highest crowds and among the highest prices. One thing to note: Central Pacific hurricane season runs June through November and peaks August through October, so keep an eye on forecasts.

Fall (late September–November)

Late September into November is the other quiet sweet spot. Summer's dry warmth largely holds, crowds drop sharply after Labor Day, and prices fall to some of the year's lowest before the holidays. The first whales start showing up in November. The tail of hurricane season lingers into November, but active storms hitting Maui directly are uncommon.

Crowds, prices, and how to choose

Peak demand and peak prices cluster around late December through March and again in mid-summer. The cheapest, quietest stretches are typically April–May and October–early December (holidays aside). Match the season to your priority: whales and surf mean winter, calm warm water means summer, and value with good weather means the shoulders. Cross-check the month-by-month weather guide and the surf-season guide to line up your dates with the right shore.

Before you go

  • Winter's big north-shore surf is beautiful and hazardous. High surf brings strong currents and shorebreak; check live conditions and follow lifeguards, and never turn your back on the ocean.
  • Hurricane season (June–November) rarely produces a direct hit, but passing storms can bring high surf and heavy rain. Monitor forecasts and heed any official warnings.
  • Seasonal averages are not guarantees. Any month can bring a wet week or a big swell, so plan flexible days rather than counting on perfect weather.
