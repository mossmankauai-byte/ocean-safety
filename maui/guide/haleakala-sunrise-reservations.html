Haleakalā Sunrise: Reservations, Timing, and What to Bring

Watching the sun come up from the 10,023-foot summit of Haleakalā is one of the signature Maui mornings, and it takes real planning. You need a reservation to drive up for sunrise, the drive is long and dark, and the summit is genuinely cold. Show up unprepared and you will spend the whole time shivering instead of watching. Here is how the system works and what to pack.

Quick facts

  • Sunrise reservations are required for vehicles entering the summit between 3:00 AM and 7:00 AM.
  • The reservation costs $1 per vehicle and is booked only at Recreation.gov.
  • Tickets release 60 days out at 7:00 AM HST, with a second batch 48 hours before.
  • The $30 park entrance fee (or an interagency pass) is separate from the $1 reservation.
  • The summit sits at 10,023 feet; dawn temperatures are often in the 30s–40s Fahrenheit.
  • No refunds are given for cloudy weather, and the reservation holder needs matching photo ID.

You need a sunrise reservation

A sunrise reservation is required for any vehicle entering the Summit District between 3:00 AM and 7:00 AM. It costs $1.00 per vehicle and is booked only through Recreation.gov. Reservations are released 60 days in advance at 7:00 AM Hawaii time, and a smaller batch of tickets is released 48 hours before each date. They go fast, so set a reminder for the release. The reservation holder must be in the vehicle with a photo ID matching the name on the ticket.

The reservation is separate from the entrance fee

The $1 sunrise reservation does not cover park admission. The park entrance fee is $30 per vehicle, good for three days, or you can use an America the Beautiful annual or interagency pass for entry. That pass does not cover the $1 sunrise reservation, though, so you still need to book that separately. There are no refunds if clouds roll in, which they sometimes do.

Timing the drive

The summit is about a two-hour drive from the Kahului, Kīhei, or Lahaina-side areas, and longer if you are starting farther out. Sunrise time shifts through the year, so check the exact time for your date and aim to be parked at least 45 minutes to an hour before, both to get a spot and to let your eyes adjust. The road climbs fast with switchbacks in the dark, so drive rested. For realistic drive times around the island, see the Maui driving times guide.

Dress for cold, not for Hawaiʻi

At over 10,000 feet, summit temperatures at dawn are often in the 30s or low 40s Fahrenheit, and wind makes it feel colder. People routinely arrive in shorts and a t-shirt and are miserable. Bring a warm jacket, long pants, closed shoes, a hat, and gloves if you have them. A blanket from your rental helps. Hot coffee in a thermos is worth the effort.

Altitude and what else to bring

The elevation gain is rapid, and some people feel lightheaded, short of breath, or headachy at the top. The Park Service advises that people with heart or respiratory conditions, and those who have been scuba diving within 24 hours, take altitude seriously here. Bring water, snacks, a headlamp or phone light, and a full tank of gas since there is none on the mountain. Download directions ahead of time, because cell service is unreliable up top.

If you miss the sunrise window

No reservation and still want the summit? You can drive up any time outside the 3:00 to 7:00 AM window without a sunrise ticket, paying only the entrance fee. Daytime views are excellent, and sunset from the summit is spectacular and needs no timed reservation. It is a solid plan B that skips the 3 AM alarm entirely.

Before you go

  • Summit cold is the most common way people get caught out. Bring a warm jacket, long pants, and closed shoes even in July.
  • Rapid altitude gain can cause dizziness or shortness of breath. Do not drive to the summit within 24 hours of scuba diving.
  • The pre-dawn drive is long, dark, and winding. Only drive it well rested, with a full gas tank and downloaded directions.
