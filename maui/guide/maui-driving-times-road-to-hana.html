Maui Driving Times: Between Towns and the Road to Hāna

Maui looks small on a map and drives bigger than you expect. It is one big volcano and one long coastline that rarely goes in a straight line, so a short hop between two beaches can eat an hour. Here are honest drive times between the places you will actually go, plus a clear-eyed look at the Road to Hāna.

Quick facts

  • From OGG: Kīhei ~15 min, Wailea ~30, Lahaina ~40, Kāʻanapali ~45, Haleakalā summit ~1.5 hr.
  • Honoapiʻilani Highway traffic can add 15 to 30 minutes during rush hours.
  • Pāʻia to Hāna is about 64 miles, 620 curves, and 59 bridges, 46 of them one lane, 2.5 to 3 hours each way without stops.
  • No gas stations between Pāʻia and Hāna, roughly 50 miles, so fill up first.
  • Waiʻānapanapa State Park requires a prepaid timed reservation for non-residents ($5/person, $10/vehicle).
  • Haleakalā sunrise needs a separate reservation on top of the $30 park entrance fee.

From Kahului Airport (OGG)

Kahului is the island's hub, and most people start here with a rental car. Rough drive times with normal traffic: Kīhei about 15 minutes, Pāʻia about 15, Wailea about 30, Lahaina about 40, Kāʻanapali about 45, and Kapalua closer to 50 to 55. The Haleakalā summit is a long climb from sea level, plan on about 1.5 hours from the airport and more from the coast.

Traffic on the Honoapiʻilani Highway between central and west Maui can add 15 to 30 minutes during the morning and evening rush. If you land late, do not underestimate the drive out to Kāʻanapali or Kapalua in the dark.

Between the resort areas

The two main lodging zones sit on opposite sides of the island. Kīhei or Wailea to Kāʻanapali runs roughly 45 to 60 minutes depending on traffic through the central plain and along the west side. Lahaina town to Kāʻanapali is only about 10 minutes. Wailea down to Mākena and Big Beach is another 15 or so past the resorts.

Because everything funnels through the isthmus between the two mountains, there is no shortcut. Budget for the full drive, and check cell coverage before you rely on live navigation on the quieter stretches.

The Road to Hāna, honestly

The Hāna Highway runs about 64 miles from Kahului to Hāna (roughly 45 miles from Pāʻia), with roughly 620 curves and 59 bridges, 46 of them one lane. Driven straight through with no stops it is about 2.5 to 3 hours each way, but nobody does it that way, and you should not either. Plan a full day, 10 to 12 hours round trip, because the waterfalls, overlooks, and short trails are the whole point.

There is no gas between Pāʻia and Hāna town, a stretch of about 50 miles, so fill up before you leave. Leave early, around 7 a.m., to get ahead of the crowds and give yourself daylight for the drive back.

Waiʻānapanapa and its reservation

The black sand beach at Waiʻānapanapa State Park is one of the marquee stops, and non-resident visitors must reserve and prepay a timed entry online before arriving. Fees are $5 per person and $10 per vehicle, and reservations open 30 days ahead. Arrive within the first 30 minutes of your slot or you can be turned away. You can check current conditions on the Waiʻānapanapa page before you go.

Driving up Haleakalā

The summit road climbs to about 10,000 feet, and the drive from the coast takes at least 1.5 to 2 hours because of the switchbacks and the elevation gain. For sunrise, you need a separate timed reservation on top of the park entrance fee. See the Haleakalā sunrise reservation guide for the full booking process and timing.

Going down, use low gear to save your brakes, and watch for cyclists and cattle on the upper stretches.

Timing tips that save your day

Fuel up the night before any early start. Give the Road to Hāna and Haleakalā their own full days rather than stacking them. If you are staying in Kīhei or Wailea and want to see the west side, go early or late to dodge the highway backup. And do not plan a tight dinner reservation on the same evening you are driving back from Hāna, the return always runs longer than you think.

Before you go

  • The Road to Hāna is tiring to drive. Yield at one-lane bridges, pull over to let locals pass, and swap drivers if you can. Do not attempt the return in the dark if you are worn out.
  • The back side of Hāna (the Piʻilani Highway through Kaupō) is rough, remote, and has essentially no cell service. Many rental agreements discourage it, so check yours and check conditions first.
  • Cell coverage drops out along much of the Hāna Highway and near the Haleakalā summit. Download offline maps and do not count on live navigation.
