What to Pack for Maui (Including the Cold Parts)

Most Maui packing lists stop at swimsuits and flip-flops, and then people freeze at the Haleakalā summit and get soaked on the Hāna side. The coast is warm year-round, but the island has a 10,000-foot volcano and a rainforest, and you will want gear for both. Here is what actually earns space in the bag.

Quick facts

  • Pack a warm jacket, long pants, hat, and gloves for Haleakalā sunrise, which can be near freezing at 10,000 feet.
  • Bring a light rain shell and quick-dry clothes for the wet east side and the Road to Hāna.
  • Only mineral sunscreen is allowed in Maui County; bring reef-safe mineral formulas plus a rash guard.
  • Water shoes protect your feet on rocky and reef entries.
  • A portable battery and offline maps cover the cell dead zones on the east side and the summit.

The cold you did not expect

The Haleakalā summit sits around 10,000 feet, and at sunrise it can hover near freezing while Kīhei is still warm and dark below. If sunrise on the volcano is on your list, pack a warm jacket, long pants, a hat, and gloves. This is the single most common packing miss on Maui. Check the sunrise reservation guide so you book the slot and dress for the summit, not the beach.

Rain gear for the east side

The Road to Hāna and the windward, rainforest side of the island are wet a lot of the year, and passing showers are normal even on sunny days. A light rain shell packs down small and keeps a waterfall stop from ending your day. Quick-dry clothes beat cotton once you are hiking muddy trails. See the Road to Hāna guide for how wet a full day out there can get.

Reef-safe mineral sunscreen

Maui County allows only mineral sunscreen (non-nano zinc oxide or titanium dioxide), and chemical formulas with oxybenzone and octinoxate are banned for sale statewide. Bring mineral sunscreen from home or buy it locally, and pack a rash guard or sun shirt to cut how much you need. The reef-safe sunscreen guide spells out what is allowed.

For the water

Many Maui entries are rocky or have sharp reef, so water shoes or reef booties save your feet. Bring your own snorkel and mask if you have them, since rentals get old and leaky. A dry bag keeps your phone and keys safe at the beach. Before you pick a spot, read the snorkel safety guide and check live conditions.

Footwear and daypack

Pack sandals for town and the beach, plus one pair of closed-toe shoes with grip for trails and lava rock. A small daypack carries water, snacks, and layers for Haleakalā or a Hāna stop. Reusable water bottles matter, hydration is easy to neglect in the heat and at altitude.

Easy-to-forget extras

Bring a portable phone battery, since cell coverage drops on the east side and you will use your phone as a camera. Download offline maps before you go, see the connectivity guide. Motion-sickness remedies help on the winding Hāna Highway. A hat, polarized sunglasses, and after-sun lotion round out the kit.

Before you go

  • Cold and altitude at the Haleakalā summit are real. Underdressing for a predawn sunrise at 10,000 feet leaves people genuinely miserable and can be a health risk for some.
  • Water shoes help, but rocky and reef entries are still hazardous in surf. Footwear does not make an unsafe ocean safe, so check lifeguards and conditions first.
  • Do not bring or use chemical sunscreens with oxybenzone or octinoxate; they are banned for sale statewide and non-mineral sunscreen is restricted in Maui County.
