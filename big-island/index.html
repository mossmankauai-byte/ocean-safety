Calmest, most reliable swims

Waialea Bay (Beach 69)
Locals call it Beach 69 for the old telephone pole marker. Reef on both ends makes it one of the best easy snorkels in Kohala on calm summer mornings. No lifeguard. Calm most mornings, but check for swell on the open-water side and expect murkier water after rain from the intermittent stream.
Kaunaʻoa Beach (Mauna Kea)
A crescent of sand fronting the Mauna Kea Beach Hotel, calm in summer. No lifeguard, and the same NW winter swell that hits Hāpuna builds a pounding shorebreak and rip currents here, so judge conditions yourself before entering and stay out when the surf is up.
Hāpuna Beach
Half-mile of white sand and the island's best all-around swimming beach in calm summer conditions — but it leads Hawaiʻi in shorebreak spinal injuries. The gentler southern end is safer. Lifeguarded daily; on any swell the dumping shorebreak can slam you onto a flat sand shelf, so check the flags and ask the tower before going in.
Richardson Ocean Park
Best snorkel on the Hilo side — cold freshwater springs feed a cove sheltered behind a black-rock barrier, and green sea turtles rest here (stay 10 ft back). Calm on normal days, but during high surf strong currents and rip can develop and the park is closed; always check with the lifeguard before entering.
Mauʻumae Beach
A hidden pocket beach reached by a short coastal trail — quiet and beautiful, calm only on flat summer days. No lifeguard and no easy exit if the surf is up, with slow emergency response this far out, so treat it as a swim-at-your-own-risk spot and skip the water when there's any swell.
Samuel M. Spencer Beach Park
The calmest, most protected swimming on the Kohala coast — an offshore reef knocks the swell down, making it the go-to for young kids and first-time snorkelers. Lifeguarded daily per the state list, though some travel sites report weekends/holidays only — if in doubt, treat midweek as unguarded and check the tower.
