Hawaiʻi Island beach conditions today

Live ocean conditions and an honest, beach-by-beach safety read for 28 Hawaiʻi Island beaches.

Kona (West)

Kahaluʻu Beach Park ⚠
County lifeguards and Fire Dept.
Laʻaloa Beach (Magic Sands / White Sands) ⚠
Severe seasonal shorebreak that dumps directly onto a steep sand face — neck, back and shoulder injuries are common (locally nicknamed 'Tragic Slams'), one of Hawaiʻi's worst beaches for spinal/neck injuries.
Kua Bay (Maniniʻōwali) ⚠
Heavy seasonal shorebreak that turns punishing in winter, plus high surf and strong currents — official condition data flags heavy shore break, high surf and strong currents ('swim at your own risk').
Makalawena Beach ⚠
Remote, unguarded white-sand beach reached only on foot (~20–30 min hike over lava) or rough 4WD.
Mahaiʻula Beach (Kekaha Kai State Park) ⚠
No lifeguard anywhere in Kekaha Kai State Park.
Hōnaunau Bay (Two Step) ⚠
No sand and NO lifeguard — entry/exit is off two lava ledges directly into deep water.
Kamakahonu Beach (Kailua Pier / King Kam)
Tiny, very calm pocket of sand beside the pier — the safest swim in Kailua-Kona town and great for small kids.
Kekaha Kai State Park (Mahaiʻula–Makalawena area; draft 'Manini Beach' label is incorrect) ⚠
No lifeguard anywhere in Kekaha Kai State Park.

Kohala (North)

Hāpuna Beach
Half-mile of white sand and the island's best all-around swimming beach in calm summer conditions — but it leads Hawaiʻi in shorebreak spinal injuries.
Kaunaʻoa Beach (Mauna Kea)
A perfect crescent fronting the Mauna Kea Beach Hotel — calm and family-friendly in summer.
Samuel M. Spencer Beach Park
The calmest, most protected swimming on the Kohala coast — an offshore reef knocks the swell down, making it the go-to for young kids and first-time snorkelers.
Waialea Bay (Beach 69)
Locals call it Beach 69 for the old telephone pole marker.
Mauʻumae Beach
A hidden pocket beach reached by a short coastal trail — quiet and beautiful, calm only on flat summer days.
Kīholo Bay ⚠
No lifeguard, remote, and no cell service — emergency help is far away.
Pololū Valley Beach ⚠
Powerful shorebreak and strong year-round rip currents on an exposed, unsheltered black-sand beach at the bottom of a steep cliff trail.

Hilo & Puna (East)

Richardson Ocean Park
Best snorkel on the Hilo side — cold freshwater springs feed a cove sheltered behind a black-rock barrier, and green sea turtles rest here (stay 10 ft back).
Carlsmith Beach Park (Four Mile)
Lava-rock breakwater forms a calm lagoon — turtles graze the inner pools and it's gentle enough for kids on normal days.
Leleiwi Beach Park (Waiʻuli)
Rocky tidepool coastline with sheltered inlets — good snorkeling when the surf is down, but entry is over lava with no sand beach, so it suits confident swimmers over young kids.
Onekahakaha Beach Park
A boulder-walled wading pool makes this the safest spot for toddlers near Hilo.
Honoliʻi Beach Park ⚠
Premier Hilo surf break, not a swimming beach.
Pohoiki (Isaac Hale Beach Park) ⚠
Brand-new (2018 lava) black-sand beach with a dangerous shorebreak and strong currents along the sand.
Kehena Beach ⚠
Fully exposed to open-ocean swell with a brutal shorebreak and rip currents/undertow that have caused MULTIPLE drownings (documented deaths 2017, 2019, 2022, 2024).

Kaʻū (South)

Punaluʻu Black Sand Beach ⚠
Despite a lifeguard tower at the park, the water here is hazardous: a strong rip runs out the boat channel/ramp on the north/northeast side, cold offshore freshwater springs cause sudden temperature shock, and high surf is common.
Papakōlea Green Sand Beach (Mahana Bay) ⚠
Remote olivine-sand cove inside a collapsed cinder cone with no lifeguard, no shade, and no rescue.
Ka Lae (South Point) ⚠
The southernmost point of the USA — lava cliffs over deep open ocean, NOT a swimming beach.

Whittington Beach Park (Honuʻapo) ⚠
A rocky lava shoreline on Honuʻapo Bay, NOT a sand swimming beach, with no lifeguard.
Honuʻapo Bay (Honuʻapo Estuary) ⚠
A restored fishpond/wetland estuary preserve on Honuʻapo Bay with a rocky shoreline and no lifeguard — for coastal walking and birding, not swimming.
