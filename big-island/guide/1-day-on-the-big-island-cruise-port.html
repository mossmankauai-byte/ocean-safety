1 Day on the Big Island from the Kona or Hilo Cruise Port

Ships reach Hawaiʻi Island two different ways, and which one you get decides your whole day. Kailua-Kona has no cruise berth, so ships anchor offshore and tender passengers to Kailua Pier in the middle of town. Hilo has the island's largest harbor, a working commercial port on Hilo Bay with nothing walkable at the gate. One drops you in a walkable town. The other does not.

Quick facts

  • Kailua-Kona has no cruise berth; ships anchor offshore and tender passengers to Kailua Pier in downtown Kailua-Kona.
  • Hilo Harbor is the largest harbor on the island, and Hilo International Airport, where the rental agencies are, sits beside it.
  • Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park is 30 miles and about 45 minutes from Hilo, but about 95 miles and 2 to 2.5 hours from Kailua-Kona.
  • Park entrance is $30 per private vehicle, credit or debit only, and the park is open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.
  • Kīlauea has erupted inside Halemaʻumaʻu since December 23, 2024, in intermittent episodes; the eruption area is closed and the Park Service warns overlook parking can be full by 10 a.m.
  • ʻAkaka Falls State Park closes at 5 p.m. and charges non-residents $5 per person plus $10 per vehicle, credit card only.
  • Rainbow Falls, in Wailuku River State Park, is 80 feet high, open 7 a.m. to 5:30 p.m., and charges non-residents $5 per person plus $10 per vehicle.
  • The Mauna Kea summit road above the 9,200-foot Visitor Information Station is 4WD only, and summit access ends 30 minutes after sunset.

Kona: you tender, and it costs you hours

The harbor at Kailua-Kona cannot take a cruise ship, so you anchor and ride a tender in. Tendering takes time at both ends of the day, and the queue home is the slower one. Subtract it from your window before you plan anything.

The trade is where you land. Kailua Pier sits in downtown Kailua-Kona, with shops and restaurants within walking distance. Kamakahonu Beach is at the pier itself, a small protected cove beside the Kamakahonu royal residence and Ahuʻena Heiau, with no lifeguard. Mokuʻaikaua Church on Aliʻi Drive is the oldest Christian church in the Hawaiian Islands, with a congregation dating to 1820. If the tender eats your morning, you can still have a real day without a car.

Hilo: you dock, but you are not downtown

Hilo ships tie up in Hilo Harbor, the largest harbor on the island. It is a commercial cargo port with nothing walkable at the gate, and historic downtown is a short drive away. The rental agencies are at Hilo International Airport, which sits right beside the harbor.

What you lose in walkability you gain in reach. Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park is 30 miles and about 45 minutes from Hilo, which is the one thing that makes a Hilo port day different from a Kona one.

The volcano from Hilo

Entrance is $30 per private vehicle, credit or debit only, and the park is open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. Kīlauea has been erupting inside Halemaʻumaʻu at the summit since December 23, 2024, in intermittent fountaining episodes rather than continuously, at USGS alert level Orange, Watch, as of late July 2026. The eruption area is closed. Viewing is from open overlooks on the caldera rim, including Uēkahuna, Kīlauea Overlook at Kūkamāhuākea, Kūpinaʻi Pali and Keanakākoʻi, and the Park Service warns those lots can be full by 10 a.m.

Budget about 90 minutes of driving round trip plus at least two hours inside the park, and check the USGS Kīlauea update the morning you dock, because an episode may not be running. If it is not, the Kīlauea Iki Trail across the crater floor and the Crater Rim Drive tour around the summit still justify the drive.

Shorter Hilo options

If the park does not fit your window, Rainbow Falls is roadside in Wailuku River State Park at the edge of town, 80 feet high with no hiking, open 7 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. with gates locking at 6, and it charges non-residents $5 per person plus $10 per vehicle. ʻAkaka Falls State Park is north on the Hāmākua coast, open 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m., $5 per person plus $10 per vehicle for non-residents, credit card only, on a 0.4-mile loop footpath with stairs.

Richardson Ocean Park and Carlsmith Beach Park are east of Hilo and both have full-time lifeguards. They are rocky reef pockets rather than sand beaches, and honu feed in them, so keep at least 10 feet of distance under NOAA guidance.

What a Kona day can actually reach

South of town, Kahaluʻu Beach Park has a full-time lifeguard and shallow reef snorkeling, though it carries a hazard flag in our conditions data for current through the reef. Laʻaloa, or Magic Sands, is also lifeguarded, but the sand strips off it in winter swell, which is how it got the name, and it carries a hazard flag too. North, Hāpuna Beach is a state recreation area on the Kohala coast with lifeguard service and a non-resident fee of $5 per person plus $10 per vehicle.

Kona coffee farms in the hills above town are a shorter option that keeps you close to the pier. Whatever you book, confirm your all-aboard time first.

What will not work from either port

Do not attempt Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park from Kona. The Park Service puts it at about 95 miles and 2 to 2.5 hours each way on Highway 11, which is roughly five hours of driving before you have seen anything. The ship will leave without you.

Mauna Kea's summit is out as well. The road above the 9,200-foot Visitor Information Station is unpaved, rough and steep, 4WD only, and summit access closes 30 minutes after sunset. Papakōlea green sand beach is about 3 miles east of Ka Lae, or South Point, at the far southern end of the island and legally reachable only on foot. Access down Waipiʻo Valley Road is limited to residents, county-permitted tour operators, and Native Hawaiian traditional and customary practitioners.

Before you go

  • Build a wide buffer for your all-aboard time. At Kona you are tendering both directions, and the queue back to the ship is longer at the end of the day than at the start.
  • Hawaiʻi Island has few lifeguarded beaches. Hawaiʻi's state ocean safety program says shorebreak is an unpredictable and dangerous condition where waves break directly onshore, that the average wave height when a spinal cord injury occurs is 3 feet, and that you should avoid going into the water at beaches with dangerous shorebreak.
  • The Park Service warns that hazardous volcanic gasses endanger sensitive groups, naming people with heart or respiratory issues, infants, children, and pregnant women. Check air quality before you drive up.
  • The Park Service tells visitors to stay on marked trails and overlooks, never enter closed or restricted areas, and keep a safe distance from earth cracks, cliff edges, vents and fumaroles.
  • Do not try the national park from Kona on a port day. About five hours of round-trip driving against a fixed departure is how people get left behind.
